The Good for Nothing Universe begins with very little.

It is an evolving meta-infrastructure for making things possible without deciding in advance what they must become.

The work begins with conditions rather than outcomes. Whether developing a program, producing an event, exploring a mathematical question, or following a creative idea somewhere unexpected, the Good for Nothing Universe is interested in what needs to be present for something worthwhile to become possible—and what can be left open.

This often means finding just enough structure to support what matters without determining everything that follows. It means paying attention to relationships, responsibilities, constraints and assumptions; noticing when something useful has become unnecessary; and building ways for people, ideas and possibilities to retain their own integrity while becoming part of something larger.

The form changes with the work. Sometimes it is a room full of people in a town hall. Sometimes it is a program, an experience, a system, a piece of writing, or a mathematical investigation. The practice underneath is similar: understand what the situation actually requires, create the conditions carefully, and leave enough space for what could not have been planned.

In this work, it is less interesting to design the answer than to make surprising answers possible amongst the people who have them.
